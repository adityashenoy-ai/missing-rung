AI and the career ladder · India edition · data: Apr–May 2026

The Missing Rung

AI isn't taking jobs evenly. In 12 early-career roles, the tasks juniors traditionally learn on are the ones people most often hand over to AI. The tasks seniors do are the ones people mostly work through with it.

What this data is. How people use Claude, measured by Anthropic across millions of conversations and mapped to U.S. Department of Labor (O*NET) tasks. It shows where AI is doing the work in practice. It does not measure hiring, job losses, or the whole labour market.

How this was built

Method, checks, and what could be wrong

Steps

  • Took every O*NET task in Anthropic's Economic Index release of 26 June 2026 (Apr–May 2026, global), averaging the two months where both are published.
  • Mapped 12 roles that early-career people in India apply for to the closest O*NET 31.0 occupations.
  • Tagged all tasks Junior, Mid or Senior from the task wording, before looking at any AI numbers. Tags and reasons are in data/ladders.csv for anyone to dispute.
  • Wrote the hypothesis down first: junior tasks show a higher automation share than senior tasks.
  • Status: broken ≥ 65% automation (roughly the top quarter of all tasks), cracking 50–65%, holding < 50%. Tasks below 0.01% of usage are shown as thin data and left out of every headline number.

What could be wrong

  • Claude users aren't a random sample of workers, and people may bring Claude the tasks they find easiest to hand over.
  • Seniority tags are judgment calls. A simple keyword tagger agrees with them on % of tasks and gives the same pattern.
  • Senior tasks rarely appear in Claude usage (), so the junior vs senior gap rests on fewer tasks than junior vs mid.
  • O*NET describes U.S. occupations. Indian job titles bundle tasks differently.
  • A check set in advance did not confirm the tags: the dataset's estimate of years of education a task needs is not lower for junior tasks. That estimate measures schooling, not on-the-job experience, but it remains a limitation.
